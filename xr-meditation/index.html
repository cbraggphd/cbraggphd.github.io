XR Meditation and Interview Skills

Five minutes of meditation improved how students deliver in interviews

We studied 242 CCSU students practicing job interviews with an AI interviewer in virtual reality. Each student did the interview twice. Between the two, some did a five-minute guided breathing meditation, either in the headset or with a live guide. Others simply sat and listened to music.

Key numbers

  • 242 students in the CCSU XR Lab
  • About 10% higher delivery scores for students who meditated (7.4 vs. 6.7 out of 10)
  • 5 minutes of guided breathing

Practice helped everyone

Every group got better the second time. Overall interview scores rose from 5.3 to 5.7 out of 10. Students also felt less anxious and came away more positive about VR as a learning tool, with attitude scores up 16%. Practice alone drives much of that.

Meditation changed how students spoke

Only the students who meditated improved their delivery: eye contact, body language, pace, clarity, and fewer filler words. Students who just rested did not. By the second interview, meditators’ delivery scores were about 10% higher than the rest group’s (7.4 vs. 6.7 out of 10). These differences were statistically significant, meaning they are very unlikely to be due to chance.

The headset meditation worked as well as a live guide, so it can be added to training without extra staff.

Why it matters

A five-minute exercise improved the interview skills employers notice first. We are now testing this directly in our next study.

About the study

Conducted by the Organizational Psychology Answers Lab (OPAL) in the CCSU XR Lab, Department of Psychological Science, Central Connecticut State University.
